Slide 5 · Strategy

From complexity to frontline value

05
Problem hook

RouteLogic’s complexity is pushing operational users into workarounds, reducing adoption and putting major customer relationships and future market share at risk.

Value proposition

For the 95% of customers who rely on RouteLogic for day-to-day operations, we will simplify core workflows and remove unnecessary complexity so users can complete time-sensitive work faster, while preserving the advanced capabilities required by high-end enterprise customers.

Slide 6 · Research

Frontline work breaks when the route cannot be trusted

06
Competitive analysis / workaround

Outside tools become the backup system

WhatsApp / textPhone callsScreenshotsPrinted route copy

Screenshot or print the route before leaving; manually override unsuitable directions; text delivery updates to the dispatcher; call the office if route information disappears; retake proof-of-delivery photos until the upload works.

Moment of misery

The process feels most broken when the driver cannot access or trust route information during an active delivery and must stop to contact the office or use a manual backup.

Evidence

“I reassign a route and the driver doesn’t see it for ten, fifteen minutes. By then they’ve driven the wrong way. We keep a WhatsApp group as the real system.”

Slide 7 · Blueprint

Prioritize broadly, scope one feature deeply

07
Now

Driver Alert Notifications

Selected as the first focused intervention to reduce reliance on calls and texts when routes change.

Next

One-Click Compliance Checklist

A high-value improvement requiring more design and engineering effort.

Later

Step Progress Indicator

Adds clarity but does not remove the underlying complexity.

Scoped feature · PRD highlights
  • Notify the assigned driver when a route changes
  • Clearly explain what changed and when
  • Link directly to the updated route
  • Remain visible until opened or acknowledged
  • Queue during temporary connectivity loss

In-app history moved to Should-Have to protect first-sprint scope.

Prototype learning

“Route changed” was too vague. Alerts must show the specific change: stop added, delayed, or cancelled.

Incomplete route updates must never trigger an alert.

Slide 8 · Validation

Validate timely route awareness

08
Hypothesis

Driver Alert Notifications will help delivery drivers become aware of route changes sooner, reducing reliance on calls and texts and the risk of following outdated routes.

Control A

The dispatcher changes a route, but the driver receives no notification. The update takes 8–15 minutes to appear, so dispatchers often use calls or WhatsApp.

VS
Variant B

The driver receives an alert showing what changed and when, then opens it to go directly to the updated route.

Bad signal

Alerts are delivered, but drivers do not open them and dispatchers continue relying on calls or WhatsApp, indicating low trust, unclear messaging, or poor fit with the driver workflow.

Decision: Expand if drivers use alerts and workarounds decrease; iterate if delivery is reliable but engagement remains low; pause if any alert reaches the wrong driver.

Slide 10 · Story

From broad problem to focused feature

10
Friction point

Several valid problems, one focused feature

The hardest part was narrowing several valid user problems into one focused feature and defining a metric that directly reflected its value.

→
Aha moment

Delivery is not the same as value

Successfully delivering an alert does not mean the feature delivers user value. The driver must see the alert in time to reduce the risk of following outdated route information.

Key takeaway

Broader initiative metrics and feature-level metrics can serve different purposes.

What I would do next

Validate the assumed baseline, run the alert experiment, and use driver and dispatcher feedback to refine the experience before expanding the launch.
